
# How to Build a Business with AI
## Detailed, Non‑Generative, Operational Guide

This document is a **reference knowledge base** for an AI Business Chatbot using **retrieval only** (RAG without generation).
It is written to be explicit, operational, and auditable. It does not assume creativity from AI systems; it focuses on **process, structure, and decision logic**.

---

## 1. Strategy: choosing the right AI business model

An AI business is fundamentally a **business first**, with AI acting as an enabling technology.
The primary strategic decision is not the model architecture, but the **value creation mechanism**.

### Common AI business models

**AI SaaS**
- Scalable, repeatable revenue
- Requires strong product discipline, uptime, and customer support
- High upfront cost, lower marginal cost

**AI-enabled services / agency**
- Faster to revenue
- Human-in-the-loop by default
- Limited scalability without process automation

**Hybrid model (services → product)**
- Services generate cash and domain insight
- Productization follows proven workflows

**Internal tools turned into B2B products**
- Often based on proprietary workflows
- Strong defensibility if tightly integrated

Strategic fit depends on:
- Access to users
- Access to data
- Sales cycle tolerance
- Regulatory constraints

---

## 2. Problem selection: defining a solvable problem

A good AI problem has:
1. A **repeatable workflow**
2. A **measurable outcome**
3. A **clear decision boundary**
4. Acceptable error tolerance

### Good AI problem examples
- Document classification
- Knowledge retrieval
- Draft generation with human validation
- Forecasting with confidence ranges

### Poor AI problem examples
- Undefined “intelligence”
- Fully autonomous decision-making in regulated domains
- One-off creative tasks with no repeatability

Problem clarity determines system reliability.

---

## 3. Data strategy: inputs, constraints, governance

AI systems operate on **data flows**, not ideas.

Key questions:
- Where does data originate?
- Who owns it?
- Who is allowed to access it?
- How long is it retained?
- How is it audited?

### Retrieval-first strategy
For many businesses, Retrieval-Augmented Systems are safer than model training:
- No model fine-tuning required
- Source traceability
- Lower compliance risk

Data quality is often more important than model choice.

---

## 4. MVP design: RAG as a first product

A minimal AI MVP should:
- Solve **one workflow**
- Use **existing documents**
- Produce **inspectable outputs**

### Typical MVP components
- Document ingestion
- Chunking by paragraph or section
- Semantic or lexical retrieval
- Evidence display (citations)

Generation is optional; retrieval alone can deliver value.

---

## 5. Validation: evidence over opinions

Validation focuses on **behavior**, not feedback.

Strong validation signals:
- Users return
- Users share real documents
- Users accept pilot constraints
- Users discuss pricing

A 14‑day validation cycle is sufficient to reject weak ideas.

---

## 6. Pricing logic for AI products

Pricing must balance:
- Customer value
- Infrastructure cost
- Predictability

### Common pricing models
- Per seat
- Per document
- Per usage unit
- Hybrid caps

Pricing must be explainable to procurement and finance.

---

## 7. Go‑to‑market (GTM)

Early GTM is manual.
Sales conversations are part of product discovery.

Track:
- Time to first value
- Activation friction
- Retention after 30 days

Marketing comes after learning.

---

## 8. Risk, compliance, and controls

AI systems must define:
- What they **cannot** do
- What requires **confirmation**
- What is logged

Risk mitigation techniques:
- Retrieval-only mode
- Source citations
- Human approval steps

Compliance is a feature, not overhead.

---

## 9. Metrics and monitoring

### Product metrics
- Retrieval relevance
- User satisfaction
- Latency
- Cost per query

### Business metrics
- Conversion rate
- Churn
- Gross margin
- Support load

Metrics guide iteration.

---

## 10. Execution roadmap (30‑60‑90 days)

**0–30 days**
- Define niche
- Build MVP
- Run pilot

**31–60 days**
- Harden onboarding
- Improve reliability
- Formalize pricing

**61–90 days**
- Scale acquisition
- Standardize operations
- Prepare compliance documentation

Execution beats novelty.

---

## Closing note

AI does not replace business fundamentals.
Distribution, trust, governance, and execution remain decisive.
